    Classical Academic System · Overview¶

    The Classical Academic System comprises two parallel routes: the Regular System and the Rudin System.
    The two routes share the same body of concepts but organize them differently: the Regular System proceeds by topic, whereas the Rudin System advances chapter by chapter along the lines of an analysis textbook.

    Two Systems¶

    System Positioning Entry
    Regular System logic, sets, number systems, algebra, spaces, branches, advanced theory Start from logic
    Rudin System an analysis route built around Rudin's Principles of Mathematical Analysis Chapter 1

    The two systems do not replace each other: the Regular System supplies the common language and structures, whereas the Rudin System unfolds the analysis portion chapter by chapter.

    Regular System¶

    Chapter Content Main prerequisites
    Logic, Language, and Proof propositions, predicates, quantifiers, proof methods none
    Sets, Relations, and Mappings set operations, relations, equivalence classes, mappings logic
    Natural Numbers and Number Systems Peano axioms, integers/rationals/reals, complex numbers logic, sets
    Algebraic Structures and Linear Spaces groups, rings, fields, vector spaces, linear maps sets, number systems
    Spaces, Limits, and Continuity metric spaces, open and closed sets, convergence, compactness, continuity number systems, algebra
    Core Branches analysis, algebra, number theory, geometry, topology, combinatorics, probability depends on the branch
    Advanced Theory measure theory, complex analysis, functional analysis, differential geometry, algebraic topology depends on the topic

    There is no rule that the chapters must be taken in sequence; the table above is merely a recommended order. Branches may be pursued in parallel, provided that the prerequisites are complete.

    Rudin System¶

    Chapter Content Status
    Chapter 1 · The Real and Complex Number Systems ordered fields, supremum, Archimedean property, n-th roots, complex numbers, Schwarz Expanded
    Chapter 2 · Basic Topology metric spaces, compactness, connectedness, Cantor set Expanded
    Chapter 3 · Sequences and Series convergence, Cauchy, limit superior and limit inferior, convergence tests for series Expanded
    Chapter 4 · Continuity limits of functions, continuity, uniform continuity, discontinuities Expanded
    Chapter 5 · Differentiation derivatives, mean value theorem, Taylor, L'Hôpital Expanded
    Chapter 6 · The Riemann–Stieltjes Integral partitions, integrability, integration by parts, rectifiable curves Expanded
    Chapter 7 · Sequences and Series of Functions uniform convergence, M-test, Arzelà–Ascoli Expanded
    Chapter 8 · Some Special Functions exponential, logarithm, trigonometric, Fourier, Gamma Expanded
    Chapter 9 · Functions of Several Variables derivatives, contraction mapping, inverse/implicit function Expanded
    Chapter 10 · Integration of Differential Forms differential forms, Stokes, Poincaré lemma Expanded
    Chapter 11 · The Lebesgue Theory measure, measurable functions, integration, L² Expanded

    "Expanded" does not mean that every theorem has been proved in full; for the status of individual proofs see Proof Status and Core Proofs.

    Recommended Reading Order¶

    1. Establish the language first: logic → sets → number systems.
    2. Then choose a structure: algebra → spaces; or proceed directly to Rudin Chapter 1.
    3. Analysis route: Rudin Chapters 1 → 2 → 3 → … → 11.
    4. Branch route: enter the analysis, algebra, number theory, geometry, topology, combinatorics, or probability material of Chapter 6 according to interest.
    5. Advanced study: enter the measure theory, complex analysis, functional analysis, geometry, and algebraic topology material of Chapter 7 according to topic.

    Page Conventions¶

    As far as possible, every page should contain:

    • prerequisites;
    • definitions and core results;
    • proofs or an explicit proof status;
    • interfaces to adjacent chapters;
    • checkpoints or exercises.

    This is an extensible knowledge map, not a verbatim copy of any textbook. The Rudin route is rewritten chapter by chapter; the Regular System is rewritten by topic.
