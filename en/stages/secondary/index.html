    Secondary Mathematics · Secondary School Stage¶

    From arithmetic towards algebra, functions and reasoning. Internally it distinguishes a "Foundation Stage" and a "Deepening Stage", corresponding to the natural transition from lower-secondary to upper-secondary knowledge, but it is not tied to the specific year of any region.

    Mathematical competitions are a separate extension module; see Mathematical Competitions.

    Module Overview¶

    Stage Study Module Main Content and Internal Order
    Foundation Rational Numbers and Real Numbers Negative numbers → the number line → absolute value → operations on rational numbers → square roots and irrational numbers → real numbers
    Foundation Algebraic Expressions Letters denoting numbers → operations on integral expressions → multiplication formulas → factorisation → fractional expressions and the conditions for them
    Foundation Equations and Inequalities Linear equation in one unknown → systems of equations → linear inequality in one unknown → quadratic equation in one unknown
    Foundation Coordinates and Introduction to Functions Cartesian coordinate plane → relations between variables → functions and their graphs → linear functions → inverse proportional functions → quadratic functions
    Foundation Plane Geometry and Proof Relations among basic figures → propositions and proof → congruence of triangles → quadrilaterals → similarity → Pythagorean theorem → circles
    Foundation Data and Randomness Data distributions → measures of central tendency and dispersion → awareness of sampling → simple random events and probability
    Deepening Sets and the Language of Logic Sets and intervals → conditions and conclusions → necessary and sufficient conditions → universal and existential statements
    Deepening Systematic Study of Functions Domain and range → monotonicity, even/odd symmetry → composition and inverse functions, preliminary → exponential and logarithmic functions
    Deepening Trigonometric Functions Similarity and trigonometric ratios in right triangles → radians and the unit circle → trigonometric functions → identities and solving triangles
    Deepening Vectors and Geometry Vector operations and coordinates → scalar product → equations of lines and circles → conic sections; spatial position and solid geometry
    Deepening Sequences and Discrete Methods Sequences → arithmetic and geometric sequences → summation → mathematical induction; counting principles → permutations and combinations
    Deepening Advanced Probability and Statistics Conditional probability and independence → discrete random variables → expectation and variance → common discrete distributions and statistical applications
    Transition Rate of Change and Intuition for Limits Average rate of change → secants and tangents → infinite approximation → first steps in derivatives and intuition for accumulated area

    "Real numbers" are understood here mainly through the number line, radicals and the properties of operations; Dedekind cuts need not be completed first; "sets" first serve as a language for expressing the range of objects, and set-theoretic axiomatics is not developed immediately.

    Foundation Stage¶

    Rational Numbers and Real Numbers¶

    • Negative numbers are quantities of opposite meaning; the number line correlates numbers with positions;
    • absolute value is the distance to the origin;
    • a rational number can be written as \(\frac pq\) (\(p,q\in\mathbb Z,\ q\ne0\));
    • \(\sqrt2\) is not a rational number (proof by contradiction: assume \(\sqrt2=p/q\) in lowest terms and derive a contradiction);
    • real numbers = rational numbers + irrational numbers; real numbers correspond one-to-one with the points of the number line (intuitive version).

    Algebraic Expressions¶

    • letters denote numbers; operations on integral expressions obey the commutative, associative and distributive laws;
    • multiplication formulas: [ (a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2,\quad (a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2, ] [ a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b); ]
    • factorisation is the reverse use of the multiplication formulas;
    • the condition for a fractional expression to be meaningful: the denominator is nonzero.

    Equations and Inequalities¶

    • linear equation in one unknown: transposition of terms, collection of like terms, reduction of the coefficient to one;
    • systems of equations: elimination by substitution, elimination by addition and subtraction;
    • linear inequality in one unknown: multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the sign;
    • quadratic equation in one unknown: factorisation, completing the square, the quadratic formula [ x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}. ]

    The discriminant \(\Delta=b^2-4ac\) determines the number of real roots.

    Coordinates and Introduction to Functions¶

    • Cartesian coordinate plane: ordered pairs \((x,y)\);
    • function: each independent variable \(x\) corresponds to a unique \(y\);
    • linear function \(y=kx+b\): the graph is a line, and \(k\) is the slope;
    • inverse proportional function \(y=k/x\): the graph is a hyperbola;
    • quadratic function \(y=ax^2+bx+c\): the graph is a parabola, with vertex and axis of symmetry.

    Plane Geometry and Proof¶

    • propositions, conditions, conclusions;
    • congruent triangles: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, HL;
    • similar triangles: AA, SAS, SSS;
    • Pythagorean theorem: in a right triangle, \(a^2+b^2=c^2\);
    • circles: central angles, inscribed angles, tangents, chords.

    Proof training begins here: every step must have a justification (a definition, an axiom, or an already-proved theorem); it is not enough that a figure looks correct.

    Data and Randomness¶

    • mean, median, mode;
    • variance/standard deviation measure dispersion;
    • sampling must be representative;
    • simple probability: number of favourable outcomes / total number of outcomes (in the equally likely case).

    Deepening Stage¶

    Sets and the Language of Logic¶

    • sets, intervals, union/intersection/complement;
    • sufficient conditions, necessary conditions, necessary and sufficient conditions;
    • universal quantifier and existential quantifier;
    • negation of a proposition.

    Systematic Study of Functions¶

    • domain, range;
    • monotonicity, even/odd symmetry, periodicity;
    • composite functions and inverse functions;
    • exponential functions, logarithmic functions, and the inverse relation between them.

    Trigonometric Functions¶

    • sine, cosine and tangent in a right triangle;
    • radian measure and the unit circle;
    • \(\sin^2 x+\cos^2 x=1\);
    • addition formulas, double-angle formulas;
    • the law of sines and the law of cosines for solving triangles.

    Vectors and Geometry¶

    • vector addition, scalar multiplication, coordinate representation;
    • scalar product \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\theta\);
    • equations of lines and circles;
    • conic sections: ellipse, hyperbola, parabola;
    • spatial vectors and solid geometry.

    Sequences and Discrete Methods¶

    • arithmetic sequences, geometric sequences;
    • summation formulas;
    • mathematical induction;
    • counting principles, permutations and combinations.

    Advanced Probability and Statistics¶

    • conditional probability \(P(A\mid B)=P(A\cap B)/P(B)\);
    • independence \(P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)\);
    • discrete random variables, expectation, variance;
    • binomial distribution, geometric distribution, and others.

    University Transition¶

    • average rate of change \(\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h\);
    • slope of a secant → slope of a tangent;
    • the intuition of infinite approximation;
    • accumulation of area and the intuition of the definite integral.

    This stage establishes intuition only; it does not replace the university \(\varepsilon\)-\(\delta\) definitions and rigorous proofs.

    Checkpoints¶

    • Can prove by contradiction that \(\sqrt2\) is irrational.
    • Can determine sufficient/necessary conditions and write down their negations.
    • Can complete a geometric proof, giving a justification for every step.
    • Can state the intuitive meaning of the derivative and of the definite integral, rather than merely memorising formulas.
