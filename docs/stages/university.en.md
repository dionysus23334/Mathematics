# University Mathematics · Undergraduate and Advanced

> We adopt "university mathematics" in the broad sense: it encompasses several directions of university mathematics, not merely the calculus course.  
> Internally it is divided into "university common foundation → disciplinary core → advanced topics"; the common foundation may be studied in parallel.

## University Common Foundation

This layer may be studied in parallel; it need not be arranged as "finish all of calculus, then study linear algebra, then study probability".

### Single-Variable Calculus

- Intuition and computation of limits;
- Continuity, derivatives, differentials;
- Mean value theorems, Taylor expansion;
- Indefinite integrals, definite integrals, the fundamental theorem of calculus;
- Simple differential equations, series.

### Linear Algebra

- Systems of linear equations and matrices;
- Vector spaces, bases, dimension;
- Linear maps, rank–nullity;
- Eigenvalues, diagonalization;
- Inner products, orthogonality, least squares.

### Multivariable Calculus

- Partial derivatives, gradients, total differentials;
- Multiple integrals, change of variables;
- Line and surface integrals;
- Green, Gauss, and Stokes theorems.

### Discrete Mathematics and Proof

- Logic, sets, relations;
- Induction and recursion;
- Combinatorial counting;
- Foundations of graph theory.

### Probability and Statistics

- Random variables, distributions, expectation, variance;
- Laws of large numbers, central limit theorem (intuitive or proved, depending on the course);
- Parameter estimation, hypothesis testing, regression.

## Disciplinary Core

### Mathematical Analysis and Real Analysis

A rigorous proof-based route is adopted: completeness of the real numbers, metric spaces, limits, continuity, differentiation, the Riemann integral, sequences of functions and uniform convergence, multivariable analysis. The Rudin System of analysis belongs here.

**Difference from common calculus**: common calculus emphasizes computation and application; analysis emphasizes definitions, proofs, and counterexamples.

### Abstract Algebra

Groups, rings, fields, homomorphisms, quotient structures, group actions, field extensions. This requires experience with sets, mappings, equivalence relations, and linear algebra.

### Foundations of Geometry and Topology

Metric spaces, topological spaces, continuous maps, compactness, connectedness, quotient spaces; an introduction to curves and surfaces.

### Differential Equations and Numerical Methods

Existence and uniqueness for ordinary differential equations, linear systems, stability; numerical approximation, error, convergence.

## Suggested Study Routes

- **Applied / engineering direction**: common calculus + linear algebra + probability and statistics + differential equations / numerical methods;
- **Mathematics major**: proceed directly to rigorous analysis + abstract algebra + topology, with the common computation courses serving as a supplement to intuition;
- **Analysis direction**: real analysis → measure theory → functional / complex analysis;
- **Algebra / number theory direction**: abstract algebra → field theory / commutative algebra → algebraic number theory;
- **Geometry / topology direction**: topology → differential geometry / algebraic topology.

## Checkpoints

- Be able to explain the difference between the common calculus course and the analysis course.
- Be able to list three prerequisites each for mathematical analysis, abstract algebra, and topology.
- Be able to explain why linear algebra can be studied in parallel with calculus.
