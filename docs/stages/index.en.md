# Educational Stage System · Overview

> Organized around how a learner comes to understand mathematics step by step: starting from concrete quantities, figures, and real problems, and gradually entering symbols, functions, change, and abstract structures.
>
> It does not replace the theoretical system; rather, the two are two ways of reading the same knowledge base.

## Three Main Entry Points

| Entry | Subtitle | Goal | Typical Starting Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elementary Mathematics | Foundational Introduction | Understand number, operations, measurement, and basic figures, and be able to explain "why the calculation works" | Quantity, counting, addition and subtraction |
| Secondary Mathematics | Secondary School Stage | Move from arithmetic to algebra, functions, geometric proof, and randomness | Rational numbers, algebraic expressions, equations |
| University Mathematics | University and Beyond | University general foundation → major core → advanced topics | Calculus, linear algebra, discrete mathematics |

"Elementary/Secondary/University" here denotes a **learning stage**, not a ranking of mathematical value; the traditional "elementary mathematics" also contains a large amount of secondary-school content, so the pages are also labelled "Foundational Introduction/Secondary School Stage/University and Beyond".

## The Four Modules of Secondary Mathematics

| Module | Main Content | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation Stage | Real numbers, algebraic expressions, equations and inequalities, introduction to functions, plane geometry, foundations of probability and statistics | Establish the foundations of secondary-school mathematics |
| Deepening Stage | Functions, trigonometry, vectors, analytic geometry, sequences, advanced probability and statistics | Systematic expansion of knowledge |
| **Mathematical Competitions** | **Geometry, Algebra, Number Theory, Combinatorics** | Topic-based exploration, problem-solving methods, and proof training |
| University Transition | Intuition for limits, rates of change, first steps in derivatives, accumulation of area | Preparation for university mathematics |

The competition module has its own entry point, but **shares the same set of prerequisite rules**: once similar triangles have been studied, one may enter introductory competition geometry; once divisibility and factors have been studied, one may enter competition number theory.

## Correspondence with the Theoretical System

| Educational Stage | Corresponding Theoretical Layer | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Elementary Mathematics | The intuitive part of number systems: "natural numbers, integers, rational numbers" | Use numbers first, do not construct them first |
| Secondary Mathematics: Foundation | Number systems within the number-system layer; preliminary set language of sets | Real numbers are first understood through the number line and operations |
| Secondary Mathematics: Deepening | Proof in logic; mappings in sets; vectors in algebra | Proof training appears gradually |
| Mathematical Competitions | Logical methods + special topics in number theory/combinatorics/geometry/algebra | Does not require completing all secondary-school content |
| University Mathematics: General Foundation | Computation and applications of layers 4–5 | May proceed in parallel with the theoretical route |
| University Mathematics: Major Core | Rigorous theory of layers 5–6 | Mathematical analysis, abstract algebra, topology |
| Advanced Topics | In depth | Prerequisites listed per topic |

## A Recommended Main Line

> Quantity and operations → symbols and relations → functions and spaces → change and randomness → abstract structures and rigorous theory.

This main line is not a mandatory queue. The university general foundation (calculus, linear algebra, probability) may be taken in parallel; a learner majoring in mathematics may choose the rigorous analysis route directly, without first completing a computation-oriented calculus course.

## Page Structure

Every stage page answers the same questions:

1. **The goals of this stage**;
2. **Module order and internal order**;
3. **What each module "should make clear"**;
4. **Articulation with the next stage**;
5. **Checkpoints**: what one can explain, compute, and prove.
