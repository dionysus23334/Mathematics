# Elementary Mathematics · Foundations and Initiation

> Objective: to understand numbers, operations, measurement and basic figures, to be able to explain the meaning of a calculation, and to solve simple practical problems.
>
> This stage does not presuppose set theory, axioms or the construction of number systems; the emphasis is on **building notions of quantity and space from concrete experience**.

## Module Overview

| Order | Module | Main Content and Internal Order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quantity and Natural Numbers | Classification and correspondence → counting → zero and the natural numbers → comparison of numbers → place value and the decimal system |
| 2 | Addition and Subtraction | Combining and increasing → decreasing and comparing → addition and subtraction as inverse operations → computation and estimation |
| 3 | Multiplication and Division | Grouping into equal quantities → multiplication → equal sharing and quotative division → multiplication and division as inverse operations → remainders |
| 4 | Laws of Operation | Commutative, associative and distributive laws → brackets → mixed arithmetic of the four operations |
| 5 | Measurement and Basic Figures | Units of length, time, mass, etc. → segments and angles → common plane figures and solid figures |
| 6 | Factors and Multiples | Divisibility → factors and multiples → odd and even numbers, primes and composites → common factors and common multiples |
| 7 | Fractions and Decimals | The unit whole and equal parts → comparison and equivalence of fractions → operations on fractions → conversion between decimals and fractions |
| 8 | Ratios, Rates and Percentages | Multiplicative relations → ratios and the unit quantity → percentages → discounts, growth and simple proportional problems |
| 9 | Perimeter, Area and Volume | Units of length, area and volume → measurement → computation for common figures |
| 10 | Introductory Algebra and Data | Unknowns and simple equalities → simple equations; classification of data → statistical charts → the mean |

These modules need not be followed in strictly serial order. Length measurement may be combined with the study of addition and subtraction; area may be understood by means of grids when multiplication is studied.

## What Each Module Must "Make Clear"

### 1. Quantity and Natural Numbers

- Counting is not the recitation of a chant but **one-to-one correspondence**;
- zero means "none", and it is also a number;
- place-value notation: the same digit denotes different magnitudes in different positions;
- comparing magnitudes: first compare the number of digits, then compare the higher places.

### 2. Addition and Subtraction

- Addition: combining, increasing, counting on;
- Subtraction: taking away, comparing, finding one part from a known sum and the other part;
- addition and subtraction are inverse operations: \(a+b=c\iff c-b=a\);
- estimation: first judge the order of magnitude, then decide whether an exact computation is required.

### 3. Multiplication and Division

- Multiplication: a concise notation for equal addends;
- Division: equal sharing and quotative division are two models;
- remainder: \(a=bq+r,\ 0\le r<b\);
- multiplication and division are inverse operations, but division by zero is meaningless.

### 4. Laws of Operation

\[
a+b=b+a,\qquad ab=ba,
\]
\[
(a+b)+c=a+(b+c),\qquad (ab)c=a(bc),
\]
\[
a(b+c)=ab+ac.
\]

Brackets change the order of operations; mixed arithmetic of the four operations proceeds by "multiplication and division first, addition and subtraction afterwards, with brackets taking precedence".

### 5. Measurement and Basic Figures

- Unit conversion: units of length, area and volume differ, and so do their conversion rates;
- segment, ray, line;
- angles: acute, right, obtuse, straight and full angles;
- plane figures: triangle, quadrilateral, circle;
- solid figures: cuboid, cube, cylinder, cone, sphere.

### 6. Factors and Multiples

- divisibility: \(a\mid b\) means that there exists an integer \(k\) with \(b=ak\);
- factors and multiples occur in pairs;
- a prime is a natural number greater than 1 whose only positive factors are 1 and itself;
- common factors, greatest common factor, common multiples, least common multiple.

### 7. Fractions and Decimals

- a fraction is "a number of equal parts taken after dividing the unit whole into equal parts";
- equivalent fractions: \(\frac12=\frac24\);
- addition and subtraction of fractions require a common denominator; multiplication is "numerator times numerator, denominator times denominator", and division is "multiplication by the reciprocal";
- a decimal is a decimal fraction; finite decimals and infinite repeating decimals can be converted into fractions.

**Why is division by a fraction equal to multiplication by its reciprocal?** Because \(\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}\) denotes "how many \(\frac{c}{d}\) are contained in \(\frac{a}{b}\)", that is,

\[
\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}=\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{d}{c}.
\]

### 8. Ratios, Rates and Percentages

- the ratio \(a:b\) expresses the multiplicative relation between two quantities;
- if both the antecedent and the consequent of a ratio are multiplied or divided by the same nonzero number, the value of the ratio is unchanged;
- a percentage is a ratio with denominator 100;
- discounts, growth rates and interest rates are all applications of percentages.

### 9. Perimeter, Area and Volume

- the perimeter is the length of the boundary; the area is the number of unit squares occupied by a plane figure; the volume is the number of unit cubes occupied by a solid figure;
- area of a rectangle \(S=ab\), of a parallelogram \(S=ah\), of a triangle \(S=\frac12 ah\), of a circle \(S=\pi r^2\);
- area formulas should be derived by dissection, subdivision or unit squares, not merely memorised.

### 10. Introductory Algebra and Data

- use letters to denote unknowns or varying quantities;
- simple equations: \(x+3=8\), subtract 3 from both sides;
- classification of data, frequency, bar/line/pie charts;
- mean = sum ÷ number of items, which is easily affected by extreme values.

## Teaching Principles

1. **Concrete before symbolic**: use objects, figures and the number line to explain operations;
2. **Meaning before algorithm**: first explain "why the computation is done this way", then practise speed;
3. **Allow informal language**: first let students explain in their own words, then gradually introduce standard terminology;
4. **Do not impose axioms prematurely**: this stage does not require set theory, the Peano axioms or a construction of the real numbers.

## Checkpoints

- Can explain why division by a fraction is multiplication by its reciprocal, rather than merely reciting the rule.
- Can explain area formulas by means of unit squares.
- Can determine whether a number is prime, and find the greatest common factor / least common multiple.
- Can read simple statistical charts and describe situations in which the mean may be misleading.
